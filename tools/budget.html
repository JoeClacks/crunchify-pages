How It Works

A budget tracker helps you understand where your money goes by recording every income and expense. Over time, patterns emerge — and awareness alone tends to reduce unnecessary spending.

The 50/30/20 Rule

A popular starting point: allocate 50% of after-tax income to needs (housing, food, utilities), 30% to wants (entertainment, dining out, hobbies), and 20% to savings and debt repayment. Use the budget limits on each category to enforce these targets.

Envelope Budgeting

The envelope method assigns every dollar a job before the month begins. Set a monthly limit per category — once a category is spent out, you stop spending there. This tool's per-category budget limits and progress bars replicate this approach digitally.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 50/30/20 budgeting rule?

Allocate 50% of after-tax income to needs (housing, utilities, groceries), 30% to wants (dining out, entertainment, hobbies), and 20% to savings and debt repayment. It's a popular starting framework — adjust the percentages to your situation.

What is envelope budgeting?

You divide your monthly income into virtual "envelopes" — one per spending category. Once an envelope is empty, you stop spending in that category. This tool's per-category budget limits replicate this approach. It forces trade-offs and makes overspending visible.

How do I track cash spending?

Log cash purchases as manual transactions immediately or at end of day — the longer you wait, the more you forget. Assign them to the appropriate category. Some people withdraw a set cash amount per week for discretionary spending and don't track individual purchases within that envelope.

Will my budget data be saved?

Yes — all data is stored in your browser's IndexedDB and persists across sessions. Use the JSON export button to back up your data regularly. Clearing your browser data or using a private window will erase it.
